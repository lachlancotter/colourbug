# Paint flow studies

These are visual directions for the painting mechanic, not screenshots or promises of the current renderer. Each uses the same indigo and dusk-violet family on warm paper so the shape of the flow is easier to compare. Generated with the built-in image generation tool.

| Study | Motion to explore | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Ink in water](ink-in-water.png) | Pigment billows beyond the brush path in translucent plumes and small rolling curls. | Buoyant, graceful, exploratory. |
| [Tie-dye front](tie-dye-front.png) | A wet front grows scallops, capillary fingers, halos, and nested colour rings. | Slow, tactile, surprising. |
| [Living droplets](living-droplets.png) | Rounded pools separate, drift, orbit, and rejoin on curved wakes. | Curious, playful, slightly mischievous. |
| [Suminagashi current](suminagashi-current.png) | A tapered ink stroke pulls fine marbled rings and quiet eddies outward into the surrounding water. | Calm, deliberate, responsive. |
| [Hybrid current and bloom](hybrid-current-bloom.png) | A flowing ink body rolls into small eddies; a few droplets peel away; the outer wet front grows tie-dye scallops and pale halos. | Buoyant, curious, tactile. |
| [Hybrid without droplets](hybrid-current-bloom-no-droplets.png) | The same flowing body, curls, and tie-dye fringe, with the detached droplets removed. | Continuous, fluid, tactile. |

The prompt set asked for one broad curved stroke per study, visible movement beyond the touched path, real liquid pigment on textured ivory paper, and no UI, hand, lettering, or literal face. The first three prompts emphasized suspended ink plumes, a tie-dye-like spreading edge, and coherent droplets respectively.

The fourth prompt draws on Japanese suminagashi floating-ink marbling and the economy of sumi ink painting. It emphasizes negative space, thin-to-thick rhythm, irregular concentric rings, and subtle currents, without pictorial motifs or calligraphy.

The fifth prompt combines the first three studies with a clear hierarchy: a coherent suspended-ink current, a few playful satellite droplets, and a tie-dye bloom confined to the wet fringe. This is a still image of the desired motion, not a screenshot of the game.

The sixth study edits the fifth with built-in image generation to remove every detached droplet while preserving its connected current, eddies, blooming edge, and paper texture.

The next design decision is which kind of motion belongs to Colourbug, and how far it should travel after the Pencil lifts. These images are still frames; the timing and response to touch need separate motion studies once a direction is chosen.
